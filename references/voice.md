# Voice from evidence

Create a small card that another GPT session can use. Include only habits that
change writing decisions; a list of generic adjectives is not a voice model.

## Card

- **Purpose and audience:** the kinds of writing these samples represent.
- **Observed habits:** directness, level of formality, contractions, sentence and
  paragraph shape, terminology, openings, requests, and expressions of uncertainty.
  Attach a short source excerpt to important observations.
- **Stated preferences:** instructions the writer gave, labeled separately from
  habits inferred from samples. Explicit preferences take precedence.
- **Keep:** distinctive turns of phrase or useful structure demonstrated in context.
- **Avoid:** demonstrated dislikes, with the situation where they apply. Do not
  turn a single example into a universal prohibition.
- **Limits:** missing genres, thin samples, mixed authorship, or conflicting cues.

A few paragraphs can support a provisional card. They cannot justify statistical
confidence, a universal fingerprint, or a promise that every future response will
sound like the writer. Prefer a relevant same-genre sample over more unrelated text.

## Applying the card

Keep the requested meaning first. Preserve the writer's regional English and
technical vocabulary. Do not manufacture quirks, fragments, typos, profanity,
first-person experiences, or claims to make writing appear human. Do not copy a
sample's anecdote into a new topic or suggest that the user personally did it.

When a requested tone conflicts with the samples, follow the request and say so
only if that distinction matters. For a voice check, show a few evidence-backed
comparisons instead of an invented similarity score.

A card returned in chat is usable in that conversation. If the user requests a
file, write it to their chosen project or personal location, outside the installed
skill directory. Never claim the model was trained or preferences were saved
across chats unless an actual supported persistence action succeeded.
